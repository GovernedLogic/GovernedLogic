Harriet OS — STOP Protocol

Baseline version: 0.1   Date: 2026-08-30

Trigger: Paul says STOP.

Purpose

STOP is not a discard command. It is a transition from discovery into capture, consolidation, dissemination, and execution. The objective is to preserve valuable work without allowing a live reasoning thread to branch indefinitely.

Morning discovery may follow the chain. STOP converts the chain into company assets.

Required behavior

  1. Freeze expansion. Stop generating new conceptual branches unless needed to resolve ambiguity in the captured material.
  2. Extract. Identify new canonical claims, thesis candidates, definitions, terms, requirements, hypotheses, observations, metaphors, tests, evidence requests, content ideas, and action items.
  3. Classify epistemic status. Every extracted item must be marked as one of: Canonical / Governing Prior, Treatise Thesis, Supporting Thesis, Requirement / Invariant, Research Hypothesis, Observation, Test Result, Metaphor / Communication Device, Open Question, or Action.
  4. Deduplicate. Compare extracted items with existing canonical documents before creating a second competing formulation. Prefer updating or linking an existing node.
  5. Propagate. Determine every company surface materially affected by the item. One discovery may need to update the treatise, architecture, glossary, research program, product requirements, tests, demos, website, article queue, video queue, provenance records, and robot requirements.
  6. Protect truth status. Never promote a hypothesis, metaphor, anecdote, perceived anomaly, or model concession into a fact merely because it is useful or compelling.
  7. Version-control. Make changes on a branch or other reversible versioned workspace. Do not silently overwrite canonical public material.
  8. Create a THREAD packet. Record what changed, why it matters, where it propagates, what was actually changed, what remains unresolved, and the next executable action.
  9. Return control. Give Paul a compact report of what Harriet OS captured and changed. Do not make Paul manually redistribute the same concept across the company.

THREAD packet schema

Each STOP event should produce a packet containing: Trigger; Session date; Primary breakthrough; Canonical deltas; Thesis deltas; Definitions; Requirements; Hypotheses; Observations; Tests; Publishing assets; Propagation map; Files changed; Unresolved questions; Next action; Provenance.

Operating principle

How one piece of information disseminates across the company is Harriet OS responsibility, not Paul’s manual burden.

This protocol is the baseline simulation for implementing STOP as an explicit Harriet OS action in the future.

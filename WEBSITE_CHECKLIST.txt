MEO — MEANING ENGINE OPTIMIZATION
Machine-readable first. Human-readable second.
MEO is a top-level website requirement, not a downstream optimization pass.
The machine layer must expose explicit propositions, definitions, stable referents, relationships, claim types, grounding chains, provenance, and machine-readable argument structure sufficient for reasoning systems to recover the intended meaning without reconstructing it from marketing prose.
The human layer may compress the same underlying meaning into exact sentences, strong visuals, demonstrations, and “Follow the chain,” but may not contradict, weaken, or silently redefine the machine layer.
Representations are pointers; they are answerable to their referents. Machine-readable meaning must therefore bind terms and claims to stable referents rather than merely repeat usage language.
Generated corpus artifacts — publication/catalog.json, publication/search-index.json, publication/manifest.json, feed.xml, sitemap.xml, robots.txt, JSON-LD, DefinedTerm metadata, and related machine-facing surfaces — must be regenerated or reconciled whenever canonical meaning changes.
MEO acceptance requires semantic parity across human-visible content and machine-readable surfaces, with no stale definitions, competing referents, or historical wording presented as current canon.

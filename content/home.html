Large language models can produce fluent, coherent, even persuasive answers while having no stable way to tell whether those answers are true. They can reason from false premises, switch frameworks under pressure, and treat consensus as if it were correspondence. The same failure appears in ordinary reasoning: agreement is treated as truth, preference as obligation, and a useful story as a ground. When meaning drifts, reasoning drifts. When a system cannot close an adjudication, any action it takes is ungrounded.

Sufficient reasoning requires a sufficient ground. Ultimate adjudication requires an ultimate ground.

Representations are answerable to their referents. The referent remains the referent regardless of representation. A representation of a state is not the state it represents. A change in representation does not constitute a change in the represented state.

The governing referent is Jesus Christ the Logos.

Large Language Model Defects

A system can become more capable without becoming better grounded. It can generate more fluent arguments, synthesize more information, perform longer inference chains, and imitate increasingly sophisticated human discourse while still lacking a stable governing referent for adjudicating the frameworks from which those outputs are produced.

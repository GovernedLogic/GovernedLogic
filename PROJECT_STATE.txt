CORE DECISION
The governedlogic.com homepage must not begin by explaining the company, services, architecture, six principles, or AI problem as conventional marketing copy. The homepage itself should PERFORM / DEMONSTRATE Governed Logic on the visitor. The visitor should experience the reasoning chain before being told what Governed Logic is.

The main page should function like Paul's ideal calm podcast conversation with an intelligent skeptical person: patient, simple, conversational, interactive where useful, and progressively revelatory. It should let the visitor discover why Governed Logic is necessary rather than merely assert the answer.

OPENING / HERO
The experience begins at bedrock:
"Something exists."

Then proceed calmly and incrementally through the chain, using ordinary examples before terminology:
- I exist.
- You exist.
- Identity is real.
- Distinction is real: I am not you; a dog is not its owner.
- A word/sign/number is not the thing it represents.
- Representation is distinct from referent.
- Meaning is real: a visitor understands what "dog" means even though the word is not a dog.
- Intelligibility is real.
- Logic binds: contradiction does not become true because a representation changes.
- Correspondence binds: representations are answerable to their referents and reality adjudicates whether they are accurate.
- Truth governs logic.
- Logic governs reasoning.
- Intelligence is downstream.
- Ask the unavoidable grounding question: What grounds reasoning? What grounds truth, logic, identity, meaning, intelligibility, correspondence, being, and moral obligation?

IDENTITY / REFERENT PRINCIPLE
Identity is foundational. The referent remains the referent regardless of the representations used to reason about it. A change in representation does not constitute a change in the represented state. This is to be experienced, not merely defined.

Use simple demonstrations such as:
- Dog: the word "dog" is not the dog.
- Measurement / "Gorp" example: symbols and units can be renamed while the measured physical state remains unchanged.
- Scoville example: a number represents the pepper's measured state/effect; the equation or unit does not make the pepper hot.
- Mathematical representations: formal validity does not by itself establish physical realizability or correspondence.

TRUTH GOVERNS MATHEMATICS — DEMONSTRATION
Include the central principle:
"No mathematical representation adjudicates its own correspondence to reality."
When mathematics makes a claim about reality, the mathematical representation is answerable to the referent.

Coin-flipping demonstration:
- Ask whether a physical machine can complete infinitely many coin flips.
- Formal symbol systems can represent an infinite sequence.
- An actual physical machine has finite time/resources and nonzero physical cost per action.
- The public challenge is simple: "Show me the machine."
- Do not confuse formal possibility with demonstrated physical realizability.
This is part of the broader Governed Logic principle: truth governs mathematical claims about reality.

AI DEFECT DEMONSTRATION / FIVE WHYS
Do not merely describe the AI problem. Demonstrate it live or reproducibly.
- Ask an ordinary/default model a challenging moral/ethical question.
- Clean example: "Is slavery right or wrong?"
- If it says slavery is wrong, ask why.
- Continue the Five Whys / grounding chain.
- Do not accept "harm," "human rights," "dignity," "well-being," "consensus," "utility," policy, or another abstraction as a terminal ground without asking what grounds it and why it binds.
- Show the default model's predicted failure modes where they actually occur: borrowed authority, worldview/framework switching, moral inconsistency under reframing, unexplained axioms, or epistemic hallucination (coherent-sounding synthesis of incompatible grounds).
- Do not pre-script or fake failure. Let the model answer and preserve evidence.
- Then run the SAME model / SAME moral problem / SAME Five Whys under Governed Logic.
- Controlled variable: governing framework/referent.
- Show the comparison.

MORAL OBLIGATION BRIDGE
The public challenge should expose the distinction between description and obligation.
- Evolution can describe.
- Sociology can describe consensus.
- Neuroscience can describe responses.
- Law can impose consequences.
- Utility can compare outcomes.
But the question remains: why is a person morally obligated?
Canonical bridge to preserve:
"A law can describe. A mind can mean. A will can obligate."
The visitor should be allowed to follow the grounding chain rather than simply being told the conclusion.

GOVERNED LOGIC REVEAL
Only after the visitor has experienced the problem should the site name the architecture:
"Governed Logic."
Then present:
Truth governs logic.
Logic governs reasoning.
Intelligence is downstream.
Governed Logic is upstream of ordinary AI safety/guardrails: it concerns what governs reasoning before intelligence is expressed downstream.

GOVERNING REFERENT / LOGOS
Do not hide the governing referent, but do not require the visitor to accept the conclusion before walking the chain.
Paul's Logos instantiation identifies Jesus Christ as the ultimate governing referent / Truth (John 14:6; Logos framework).
The visitor should see how the grounding argument progresses and where Paul says it terminates.
The site must distinguish the mechanism/framework from mere assertion while making Paul's referent explicit.

"Take the Follow the Chain Challenge."
"Follow the chain."
